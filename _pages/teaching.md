Computer Architecture
======
*Teaching assistant, Isfahan University of Technology,  Winter 2026*

Teaching assistant for the Computer Architecture course, working
with a cohort of 60 students.

I designed the homework sets and built the course project, in which students
take an existing single-cycle RISC-V core and extend it into a pipelined design
with forwarding. Beyond that, students chose one of two directions: raising
performance by making the core dual-issue, or lowering cost by narrowing the bus
to 16 bits. Framing it that way meant the project ended in a design trade-off
rather than a single correct answer, and students had to justify what they gave
up for what they gained.

I also led monthly problem-solving sessions on microarchitecture and RTL
implementation, and worked with students individually through the semester.

Research project
======
*AI-Based Trading, France (Remote), Summer 2025*

Investigated whether technical indicators and price-cycle structure could predict short-term movement in a stock's price.

I built automated data pipelines across four timeframes, extracting over 200,000 historical data points, and engineered a library of 20+ technical indicators alongside custom causal smoothing filters. I then designed and compared five trading strategies, including slope-based cycle detection, MACD-based signals, and an LSTM sequence model, validating each through multi-window backtesting against a fee-adjusted Buy & Hold benchmark. Alongside this, I ran a seasonality study across 25 years of price history, including outlier detection to separate genuine market moves from noise.

The real challenge was rarely the modeling itself, but everything around it: pulling meaningful signal out of noisy, imperfect market data, and searching for approaches actually suited to the environment, where the same method could behave very differently depending on the stock, the timeframe, or the general market conditions at the time. It left me with a much sharper sense of how to test whether a pattern is genuinely real, rather than just building something that looks convincing on paper.

Basic Programming Lab
======
*Teaching assistant, IUT Programming Lab,  Fall 2024*

Taught two sections of the introductory C programming lab in the same term, one
for computer engineering students and one for electrical engineering students,
with around 20 students each. The two groups came to the material with different
backgrounds and different reasons for needing it, which meant adjusting how much
of the systems-level context to draw on when explaining the same idea.

Most students were writing their first programs, so much of the work was less
about C itself than the habits underneath it: reading an error message carefully,
narrowing down where a program actually goes wrong, and building the expectation
that a bug has a findable cause. I designed and marked lab exercises aimed at the
concepts students most often got stuck on, pointers and memory in particular.
